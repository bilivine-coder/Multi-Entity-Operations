01   The loop

A DMCC renewal on one entity's clock. A Tawazun conformity file on another's. A bank check that can't move until you've signed it. The portals aren't the problem. The tracking is.

The first cycle of Zero Bureaucracy removed over 4,000 procedures and simplified 1,600 more — roughly 12 million hours taken off customers. That work is genuine, and it means the paperwork itself keeps getting easier. What it doesn't touch is the part that sits on your desk: knowing what's due, in which entity, under which regulator, and in what order — with negotiations, banking and studies arriving in the same queue.

03   How it works

Four gaps, in the order they usually hurt.

01

One register, one ladder

Every dated obligation across every entity and regulator in a single register. Then it remembers on its own:

90dOwner named
60dPapers requested
30dEscalated
7dNamed person

Nothing renews late because it lived in one entity's sheet.

02

Approvals that arrive decision-ready

Nothing reaches your desk as a question. It arrives with the paper, the comparison and the recommendation already attached — so the only step left is the signature, not the assembly.

03

Licensing on its own track

Defence and free-zone licensing move on different clocks from commercial filings. Tawazun conformity, Abu Dhabi licensing and DMCC renewals each get their own track, their own owner and their own escalation.

04

Reporting that exists before the question

Board, bank and investor packs draw from the same register as the renewals — so the picture is current when someone asks, not assembled after they've asked.
